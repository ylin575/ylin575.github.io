Yong Lin, PhD

Thymic epithelial cell biology, central tolerance, and T cell development

Abstract

I study how thymic epithelial cells shape T cell development and central immune tolerance — the process by which the body teaches developing T cells to distinguish self from non-self. My work combines single-cell transcriptomics with flow cytometry to characterize human thymic tissue at a resolution not previously achievable, with the broader goal of understanding how failures in this process contribute to autoimmune disease.

I'm currently a postdoctoral fellow in the Meyer Lab at the Simons Center for Quantitative Biology, Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory.

Current Work

Isolation and Characterization of Human Thymic Epithelial Cells

In Review

Journal of Immunology1

A reproducible protocol for isolating live thymic epithelial cells directly from primary human tissue, combined with single-cell RNA sequencing to resolve epithelial subsets — including a previously uncharacterized population — within the human thymus.

seq-to-flow

Ongoing

Computational workflow

A workflow that translates single-cell RNA-seq data into flow cytometry panel design recommendations — surfacing which markers actually distinguish cell populations, and at what expression thresholds. First applied to human thymic epithelial cell data.
