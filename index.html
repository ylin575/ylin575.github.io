About

I am an immunologist studying how the thymus teaches developing T cells to tolerate the body's own tissues. My research focuses on thymic epithelial cells, the specialized cells that present self-antigens to developing T cells, and on how failures in this process contribute to autoimmune disease.

I am currently a postdoctoral fellow in the Meyer Lab at the Simons Center for Quantitative Biology, Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory, where I characterize human thymic epithelial cells using flow cytometry, FACS sorting, single-cell RNA sequencing, and in vitro culture, and help lead a human thymus biobanking effort supporting a large-scale study of T cell development.

I completed my PhD in Immunology at Baylor College of Medicine, where I studied hybrid insulin peptides in type 1 diabetes using the NOD mouse model. Before graduate school, I studied Engineering Science at the University of Virginia and worked in biomaterials and neuroscience research labs.
